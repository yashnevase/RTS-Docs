# 00 — Vision and design pillars

## Working pitch

A stylized, atmospheric PC strategy sandbox inspired by the approachable resource-and-army loops of classic RTS games, the satisfying settlement development of economic builders, and the emergent political life of a medieval/early-modern sandbox. The player begins as a young heir to a once-influential Deccan clan. With only a modest inheritance and reduced authority, the player must decide whether to serve a Sultan, rebuild the clan, become a mercenary or outlaw, establish an independent kingdom, or pursue greater sovereignty. Villages are inhabited communities with needs and loyalties—not empty tiles or interchangeable resource nodes.

**This is a working pitch, not final marketing copy.**

## Player fantasy

“I inherited a fragile position in a living, politically complicated world. My choices help determine whether my family survives, my people prosper, my neighbors become allies or enemies, and whether I remain a vassal or rise to rule.”

## Intended emotional range

1. **Peaceful satisfaction:** build, improve, trade, watch work happen, and enjoy atmosphere and music.
2. **Readable action:** command forces, defend a settlement, raid, maneuver, besiege, and fight over meaningful objectives.
3. **Political agency:** relationships, promises, reputation, favors, ambition, and allegiance matter.
4. **Emergent stories:** NPCs make understandable but not fully predictable decisions; the player can recover from setbacks but can also lose power.
5. **Approachable surface, deep systems:** players can understand immediate needs and goals without needing to master every formula.

## Design pillars — proposed

- **The world is inhabited.** Capturing land means governing people, loyalties, livelihoods and local power—not merely changing a map color.
- **Power is interconnected.** Population supports production and recruitment; production supports armies; geography affects trade and war; politics affects whether forces can safely be used.
- **The player's path is open.** Service, independence, trade, conquest, mercenary work and outlaw paths are possible ambitions, subject to later scope decisions.
- **Geography matters.** Passes, hills, rivers, roads, coasts, weather and distance should create decisions, not just decoration.
- **CPU behavior has reasons.** Decisions arise from goals, personality, information, relationships, constraints and opportunities. Randomness adds variation but does not replace reasoning.
- **A polished, cohesive experience beats raw feature count.** Animation, sound, readable UI, frame pacing and feedback are part of the design.

## Audience and modes — intended outcomes, exact feature set OPEN

- Accessible enough for people who enjoy settlement-building and straightforward competitive games but do not identify as strategy experts.
- Deep enough for players who enjoy long campaigns, economic optimization, diplomacy and military planning.
- A long-session, low-pressure play style should coexist with focused battles and shorter competitive play. Whether these use separate modes/rulesets is OPEN.
- Single-player CPU campaign is the first delivery priority. Cooperative and competitive multiplayer are future goals, not assumed v1 features.

## Tone and visual ambition

Aim for a beautiful, readable, stylized 3D strategy world with a three-quarter/isometric-like camera, coherent regional architecture, atmospheric natural lighting, believable movement, satisfying combat feedback and a memorable soundscape. The Three.js screenshot/prototype is a visual reference, not a final quality ceiling. “AAA feel” means strong cohesion and polish; it does not imply AAA asset volume or production budget.

## What success should feel like

A player can understand the first useful choice quickly, care about the settlement and its people, experience consequences from a decision, and later tell a story about how they rose or fell in a world that appeared to act for its own reasons.
